    Definitions

    One definition per metric

    Each metric is calculated in one place. The tiles, charts, scorecard, summary and answers all read that same calculation, so a number cannot disagree with itself.

    How each number is defined

    How the words stay tied to the numbers

    • Every number in a summary or an answer is computed first, then put into words. Nothing is estimated in the wording.
    • "Ask the data" matches your words against a fixed list of markets, property types, metrics and question types. When a question falls outside that list, it says so and shows what it can answer.
    • The forecast is a damped trend model with an error band. It is a transparent baseline, not a market call.
    • A market is flagged as unusual when a quarter's vacancy change is 2.5 or more standard deviations from that market's own history.
    • Everything runs in this page. Nothing you type is sent anywhere.

    About the data

    • The market and lease figures are synthetic, produced by a seeded generator so the same numbers appear on every visit.
    • The market names are real places. The vacancy, rent, cap rate, construction, sales and lease figures attached to them are invented.
    • The 10-year Treasury line is based on the published history of that rate. It is the one series here that is not invented, and it is not a live rate feed.
    • The series are internally consistent: inventory grows by deliveries, and net absorption is the change in occupied space.

    Market Pulse is a demonstration. Do not use these figures for decisions about real property.

    What goes into yours.

    The demo is about commercial property because the numbers are easy to follow. The same parts go into a reporting system for any business.

    1. One definition for each number.

      Every figure is defined once, in writing, so the chart, the summary and the answer to a question all agree. The definitions are on the page.

    2. Your data streams in one place.

      Sales, bookings, costs, tickets or leases, brought together from the tools you already run and refreshed on a schedule.

    3. Questions in plain English.

      Ask what changed, which is highest or what to look at first. The answer shows the figures it used, and the dashboard moves to match.

    4. A digest and an alert.

      A short summary on a schedule, and a message when a number moves past a line you set.
