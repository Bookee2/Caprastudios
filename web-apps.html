What a build includes.

The application does what your business needs it to do. These six things come with every one.

  1. Designed around the people who use it.

    We start from the job each person has to get done and put the first working screens in front of them early, so the design is tested on real use.

  2. Your data and your accounts.

    Sign-in, roles and records, with your existing spreadsheets and exports brought in so nobody starts from an empty screen.

  3. Connected to what you already run.

    Your CRM, accounting, calendars, email, payments and files. If a system has an API or an export, the application can read from it and write back to it.

  4. AI where it earns its place.

    Search across your records, drafting, summaries, or an assistant that answers from your own data, built to the same standard as our AI systems.

  5. Tests, so changes are safe.

    Automated tests run on every change. TrailGoat has more than 2,800 of them, which is why it can change every week without breaking.

  6. Yours at the end.

    The code in your repository, the accounts in your name, and a written handover. You can run it without us.

How a build goes.

One small team, from the first question to the handover.

  1. Sprint

    Agree the job.

    A working prototype on your data, a written scope and a fixed quote. You see the idea running before you commit to it.

  2. Build

    See it working every week.

    We build in short steps and show you each one. You use the real thing as it grows, and the scope only changes when you say so.

  3. Hand over

    Launch, then keep it healthy.

    We deploy it, train the people who use it and hand over the code and accounts. Care is there if you want us to keep watching it.

Good questions.

Can you work with the software we already have?+

Yes. Most builds connect to what is already there: a CRM, accounting, calendars, email, payments, spreadsheets, or anything with an API or an export. We look at what you run before we propose anything new.

Who owns the code?+

You do. The code lives in your repository and the accounts are in your name from the first week. You get a written handover, and you can run or change the application without us.

What does it cost after launch?+

Hosting for most small applications is a few dollars to a few tens of dollars a month, paid by you to the provider. Care is optional and quoted monthly: monitoring, fixes, small changes and a report.

Can you add AI to an application?+

Yes, where it earns its place: search across your records, drafting, summaries, or an assistant that answers from your own data. It is built the same way as our AI systems, with a test set and approvals for anything consequential.

How long does a build take?+

Most applications take six to twelve weeks after the sprint. The sprint tells you the scope, the price and the date before you commit to the build.
