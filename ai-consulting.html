    Three steps, starting small.

    Our AI work is led by a Six Sigma Green Belt. We run a rollout the DMAIC way: define the problem, measure the baseline, analyze, improve, and put controls in place so the gains hold.

    Good questions.

    Is a five-person company too small for this?+

    No. A second brain pays for itself with one person using it, and a small team feels the difference fastest. Most small firms start with one second brain and one automated workflow.

    What is the difference between a second brain and a third brain?+

    A second brain belongs to one person or one team: their notes, documents, decisions and meetings in a knowledge base they can ask. A third brain is the shared version for the whole company, with permissions by role, and the second brains feed into it. Most firms start with one second brain.

    What happens to our data?+

    Your documents and records stay in accounts you own. We build on commercial AI terms that exclude training on your data, keep personal information out of logs, and write down how each system handles data before it goes live.

    What does it cost to run?+

    For most small-business systems, less than $100 a month in AI and hosting fees, paid by you directly to the providers. We tell you the expected monthly cost before we build and set a cap with you.

    What if the pilot fails?+

    Then it fails on a date, with numbers, for a fixed price, and you keep the measurements, the test set and the roadmap. Every pilot has a pass mark and a stop point agreed before we build.

    What happens when the AI model changes?+

    AI providers retire models on a schedule, with notice. Every system we build has a set of test cases, so a replacement model is checked before the switch.

    Do we need to replace our current software?+

    Rarely. We start with what you already use and what it can connect to. The right plan may connect existing tools, add a second brain, or build one focused piece.
