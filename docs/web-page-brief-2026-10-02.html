3. What to lead with instead of the turntable

A. Two lights, one lens recommended

A framed shot of this site's homepage in daylight. Move your pointer over it and the lamp goes out under your hand: the same page, in black light, shows through a soft-edged lens. On a phone it's a drag handle that sweeps the dark across. Caption: "Same site, two moods. This one's ours."
  • For: says "personality" without a word of explanation; proves we built the thing we're selling; no GPU, loads instantly; works with touch and keyboard; it's the flashlight study you already approved, promoted to hero.
  • Against: the screenshots need re-capturing when the homepage changes. We'd script the capture so it's one command.

B. The word with personality

The headline's last word changes voice each time you hover or tap it: hand-lettered, serif, mono, ribbon-blue, lime highlight. Five voices, one word.
  • For: cheapest to build; the idea and the copy are the same thing; tiny, so it never competes with the content.
  • Against: a small moment for a hero; the opener would still need a picture beside it.

C. A site reel in a browser frame

Short screen recordings of real pages (this site, the Marketing Room, TrailGoat) play in a drawn browser frame with a hand-written "this one's ours" label.
  • For: reads as a portfolio, which is what a prospect wants to see.
  • Against: video weight on the opener; needs re-recording when pages change; feels more like an agency showreel than a Capra move.

D. Stir the ink

Promote the ink GPU piece to the hero.
  • For: the most tactile piece you have.
  • Against: it's already on the homepage carousel; and it says "motion" again, which is the thing this page is stepping back from.

E. The poster wall

Still screenshots pinned at slight angles, like the marketing section lower down, with the lamp toggle flipping all of them at once.
  • For: zero motion, pure proof, loads instantly. Also the natural motion-off state for option A.
  • Against: quieter than the rest of the site; no signature moment.

The turntable goes. The shards film (the tile that breaks and rebuilds) stays, in the motion lab.

Five rules every site is built to

It has a job. Every page points somewhere: what to read, where to look, what to do next.
Quick on a phone. If it isn't fast on a mid-range phone on a weak signal, it isn't done.
Found on Google. Search set-up is part of the build, not an extra.
Works for everyone. Keyboard, screen reader, motion off: same content, same controls.
One signature moment. One thing people remember. Everything around it stays quiet.

Questions people ask first

How long does a website take? Four to eight weeks for most, depending on how many pages and how much is already written.
What does it cost? Quoted per project after a conversation. We don't publish invented prices.
Do you build on WordPress, Squarespace or Shopify? By hand, by default. If you need a shop or a team updating content daily, we choose the simplest tool that fits and set it up properly.
Can we update it ourselves? Yes. Either a simple editor for the parts that change, or a monthly hour where we do it for you.
Who writes the words? You know the business; we know what reads well on a screen. Usually both of us.
Where does it live? Fast static hosting is often free. We set it up in your accounts, not ours.
