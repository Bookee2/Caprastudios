01 / The positioning

Make the offer easy to buy.

The unifying idea is a business that looks distinctive and works well online. Motion earns attention; thoughtful websites and applications give that attention somewhere useful to go.

RECOMMENDED POSITIONING

Capra Studios is an independent design and web studio creating distinctive websites, useful web applications, and 3D animation for brands and businesses.

01 / WEBSITES

A better presence.

Buyer: a founder or business whose current site no longer reflects the quality of their work.

Offer: message, site structure, design, development, and launch. Add a brand film when it serves the story.

Proof: Capra HR and the redesigned Capra site.

02 / WEB APPLICATIONS

A more useful product.

Buyer: a product founder or operator with a specific workflow to improve.

Offer: user journeys, interface design, a working application, testing, and handoff around a defined first release.

Proof: TrailGoat and Purple Squirrel.

03 / 3D & MOTION

A brand with movement.

Buyer: a business, product team, or creative partner preparing a launch or campaign.

Offer: logo films, 3D sequences, and web/social edits with a storyboard and agreed delivery formats.

Proof: the supplied TrailGoat film, the existing unicorn film, and Atlanta flyover.

How to sell it

Lead conversations with the project: “What are you launching?” Make each service bookable on its own. Recommend a combined website and motion engagement when one idea should carry across the launch.

Keep direct access to Kris, visible progress, and clearly scoped proposals. Let the portfolio justify the quality. Avoid anchoring the studio around cheap production or an unverified promise of faster delivery.

The audience recommendation

Prioritize independent businesses and growing product teams that value both design and implementation. Keep Atlanta as a useful location cue, while leaving the work open to remote clients.

This is a positioning hypothesis, not market validation. Use the next few qualified conversations to learn which offer brings the strongest briefs before narrowing further.

Visual direction / Recommendation

Quiet interface. Expressive work.

04 / The unicorn film

From blocks to character.
From character to signature.

The supplied TrailGoat film is the creative reference. The next step is a Capra adaptation using your forthcoming 3D unicorn model, preserving the approved ribbon identity.

View the sampled TrailGoat sequence

Frames sampled at two-second intervals, read left to right across the top row, then the bottom row. Use the player for motion and pacing.

What makes it work

The blocks have physical volume and catch the light. The character reveal creates a recognizable payoff. The flip gives it personality; the wordmark finish connects the spectacle to the brand. The dark stage keeps the silhouette central.

The contact sheet and sampled frames establish this sequence. Playback in the plan is available for full-speed review; they do not establish frame-by-frame motion quality.

What changes for Capra

Use blue-violet surfaces and a controlled rim light. Make the horn, face, and ribbon-shaped neck readable before the movement accelerates. Prefer a graceful turn or controlled flip; choose the move in the animatic based on the new model’s proportions.

Keep the final wordmark clear for roughly two seconds. Put the site’s real headline and calls to action in HTML, outside the video.

Proposed 12-second web edit / Not yet rendered

00–02 SEC

Set the stage.

Begin with a clean, complete composition. A lit cluster of blue-violet blocks enters above a dark surface.

02–05 SEC

Find the form.

The pieces separate and gather into the unicorn. Reveal the horn and open neck silhouette clearly.

05–08 SEC

Give it life.

A controlled flip or turn, then a calm settle. Preserve the silhouette through the rotation.

08–10 SEC

Make the mark.

The form breaks into pieces and resolves into the Capra Studios signature. Avoid a crowded particle cloud.

10–12 SEC

Let it land.

Hold the finished wordmark. End deliberately and offer replay. No required endless loop.

Production brief / Tomorrow or later

Model → look → movement → finish.

  1. Receive the unicorn model. Check silhouette against the existing vector, mesh quality, normals, scale, pivot, materials, and use rights.
  2. Once the shared Unreal session is free, inspect the actual project and MCP capabilities. Use a separate level or project copy and preserve Claude’s scene.
  3. Create a still look-development frame and low-cost animatic first. Test how the pieces reveal and hold the unicorn before choosing the final flip.
  4. Build the shot sequence and repeatable particle or instanced-block setup using tools the installed version actually supports. Bake/cache once the motion is settled.
  5. Render approved cameras to image sequences. Encode web and social variants directly from those frames; inspect the first, middle, and final frames plus full-speed playback.

Unreal MCP: no connection attempted. Available controls and exact implementation remain unverified. Blender can prepare geometry and assets; the future session determines the actual division of work.

Delivery and website behavior

A complete viewing experience.

  • Target master: 1920 × 1080 or higher, 30 fps. Optional longer 18–20 second portfolio cut.
  • Separate 1:1 and 9:16 compositions for phone/social; keep horn and wordmark within safe framing.
  • Finished poster before any request. Click-to-play as the baseline; any optional autoplay stays silent, finite, and preference-aware.
  • Visible play, pause, and replay; pause offscreen and on hidden tabs; respect a manual pause.
  • Reduced motion, data saving, blocked autoplay, or media failure leaves useful still artwork.
  • Test byte-range delivery, phone playback, file size, and readability. Proposed budgets: ≤5 MB desktop and ≤3 MB phone for the short edit, subject to visual quality review.
  • Sound is a separately scoped choice for the portfolio/social cut. The website must work silently.

Art direction before repetition.

A practical brief / Adapted from RISE

Reference. Idea. Style. Examine.

Reference: your actual TrailGoat block film, the Capra vector silhouette, and the approved unicorn still.

Idea: pieces become an unmistakable unicorn, move with intent, and settle into Capra Studios.

Style: blue-violet material, a dark stage, physical lighting, clean typography, and deliberate pacing.

Examine: recognizable silhouette, no stray or popping pieces, readable wordmark, first-to-last continuity, phone-safe framing, and an effective silent edit.

Observable acceptance criteria

  • The opening names the studio category and all three capabilities without relying on video.
  • The sales copy contains no AI-first positioning, training offer, or tooling explanation.
  • Every showcased project states the role, actual deliverables, and whether it is owned or commissioned work.
  • A visitor can move from each offer to relevant work and a project inquiry.
  • Layouts inspected at 1440, 768, 390, and 320 pixels, with no unintended horizontal scrolling.
  • Typography loads correctly; links and controls have visible focus and a 44px touch-target goal.
  • The film supports manual control and static fallbacks. Important information survives JavaScript being unavailable.
  • Current tests, metadata, contact delivery, asset sizes, and actual deployment behavior are verified during implementation.

Scope boundary: this delivery is the researched HTML plan. A full site rebuild, new logo model, Unreal render, pricing commitments, deployment, and recurring automation are outside today’s work. Timelines beyond tomorrow’s earliest production start depend on the model, project scope, and review.
