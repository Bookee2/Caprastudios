Five directions worth exploring

01 / First choice · Hero

A unicorn woven from light.

For Capra: luminous strands follow the ribbon silhouette, separate as the pointer passes, and settle back into the mark. Cyan and violet carry the shape; magenta and amber pulses travel through it. The idle loop is a slow current.

Reference: Mathis Biabiany’s lit GPU tubes give strands volume and lighting. Its implementation uses WebGPU/TSL; the author explicitly describes the ideas as portable to GLSL.

Explore Luminhands ↗ · Author’s breakdown ↗

Proposed WebGL adaptation, not a drop-in WebGL demo. Begin with curves from the existing mark; a later 3D model can supply depth. Medium–high effort.

02 / Material study · Hero alternative

Prismatic glass. Liquid chrome.

For Capra: an apparently solid unicorn turns just enough to catch a hot pink edge, a cyan interior, and an amber glint. A restrained pointer tilt makes the material feel tangible. Explore glass and chrome as separate treatments.

References: Frank Reitberger’s Volatile Nexus combines an iridescent glass ring with moving cubes. Three.js provides transmission, dispersion, and iridescence for a model-based WebGL treatment.

Explore Volatile Nexus ↗ · Three.js dispersion demo ↗ · Material documentation ↗

Needs a suitable optimized 3D asset. Glass increases rendering cost; reflections require art-directed lighting. High effort. Nexus advertises sound on first interaction.

03 / Recommended · Selected work

Projects woven into the next.

For Capra: a project image briefly separates into ribbon strips, then gathers into the next project. It connects the unicorn’s construction to the work itself. Keep titles, links, and project information as readable HTML.

Reference: Clément Grellier’s Unwoven presents images as an animated loom in Three.js/WebGL.

Explore Unwoven ↗

WebGL reference. Trigger on deliberate gallery navigation; don’t continuously distort the portfolio. Medium effort; no new 3D model required.

04 / Recommended · Interactive detail

Color waiting beneath the surface.

For Capra: the cursor leaves a brief wake of cyan, magenta, and orange across a frosted ribbon sculpture. The color then softly recedes. A tap can trigger a small contained response on touch screens.

Reference: Sujen Phea’s glass xylophone uses a simplified fluid field to reveal a vivid gradient through frosted glass, alongside interactive movement.

Explore the glass xylophone ↗ · Author’s breakdown ↗

WebGL reference. Place in one feature area or motion-service artwork. No sound needed for Capra. Medium–high effort.

05 / Supporting technique · Playground

Ink that has momentum.

For Capra: colored fluid flows within the unicorn or through a contained experimental canvas. Pointer movement imparts momentum, so the color continues moving after the hand stops.

Reference: Pavel Dobryakov’s WebGL Fluid Simulation is a useful established technical foundation. The experiment dates to 2017; it is not a new 2026 breakthrough.

Explore fluid simulation ↗ · Author’s source ↗ · Original experiment date ↗

WebGL reference. Strong supporting behavior; less distinctive as a whole-screen background. Medium effort, with simulation resolution tuned per device.

Recommended composition

One unmistakable signature.
A few moments of discovery.

Build the filament unicorn first. Carry its ribbon language into project transitions. Use the glass color reveal as a smaller interactive surprise.

Let the hero own the brightest color. Match gallery accents to the project being shown. Give service loops quieter versions of the same palette. Keep the closing signature simple enough to leave the contact invitation clear.

What makes it feel advanced.

Material

Surfaces respond convincingly to light. The color has a reason: refraction, an illuminated strand, or a moving pigment.

Response

Interaction has momentum and a graceful recovery. Mouse, touch, and keyboard visitors can all understand the work.

Composure

Content appears immediately. Motion rests, pauses offscreen, and has a deliberate reduced-motion composition. Detail holds up on a phone.

The next experiment should compare the current particle study with one colorful filament treatment using the same Capra silhouette. Judge recognizability, visual impact, pointer response, and actual device performance before adopting it.

Evidence & scope

Primary author tutorials and Three.js documentation informed the technical assessment. Unwoven, Volatile Nexus, and the glass xylophone were opened in Chrome and their desktop renders inspected. Nexus rendered its scene but retained a “loading…” overlay in the captured frame. These checks do not establish mobile performance or complete interaction reliability. Luminhands was assessed through its author’s explanation, not a verified local run.

WebGL feasibility for proposed Capra adaptations remains to be proven in a prototype. No Unreal connection was made. No production website changes or deployment are included in this research.
