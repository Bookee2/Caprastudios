Capra Studios, in plain facts.

What we make, who it is for, what it costs and what you can check for yourself. One page, written for people and for the AI assistants they ask.

Updated November 2026.

  1. The studio

    Capra Studios (Capra, LLC) is an independent land marketing studio in Atlanta, Georgia, led by Kris Brown.

    It makes marketing films for land. The person who scopes a film is the person who builds it.

    About →

  2. Land marketing filmsFor brokers, owners, investment managers and developers

    A land marketing film makes the case for a property: its strengths shown one at a time on the real ground, in the client’s brand, ending on the client’s name and call to action.

    • For a listing: a tract’s strengths shown to buyers who cannot walk it.
    • For a fund or a portfolio: the kind of property held, shown to investors, with the client’s disclosures on screen word for word. The property can stay anonymous.
    • For a planned project: the site as it stands and as it is planned, for investors, neighbors and the boards that have to approve it.

    The land is built from public lidar and aerial photos, so the ground and the trees on screen are the real ones. A film is an illustration, not a survey, a timber cruise or an appraisal.

    The films →

  3. What comes with a film

    • The film, about 80 to 130 seconds, in 16:9.
    • A 4:5 cut and a vertical cut for social feeds.
    • Stills at full resolution.
    • The client’s colors, typefaces, logo and wording, including disclosures.
    • On request, a version with place names, roads and landmarks left out.

  4. Kinds of land

    Timber and investment tracts, hunting and recreational land, farms and pasture, waterfront and mountain land, plantations and conservation land, and development land and planned projects: residential communities, commercial and industrial sites, and data centers.

  7. How a film is made

    • The client sends parcel IDs, a listing link, a survey or a site plan.
    • Capra checks the land against the public records and agrees what the film has to say before the client commits.
    • The client marks up a draft: the wording, the shots and the order.
    • A single tract takes about a week. Nobody has to visit the land.

  8. What Capra does not promise

    • A film is an illustration. It is not a survey, a timber cruise or an appraisal.
    • Property lines come from the county’s parcel map unless the client sends a survey, and are labeled approximate.
    • Capra does not review legal or securities compliance. That is for the client’s counsel.
    • MLS rules on rendered media differ. A version is prepared only where an MLS allows it.
    • No guaranteed timelines. The timing is agreed before work starts.
